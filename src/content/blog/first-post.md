I've had several blogs during my time here on Earth. Sharing ideas, information, and interesting notions is a great motivating factor behind a blog. In the era of mono-formst social media platforms, stripped of personality and filtered for corporate interests over those of the common man and woman, it's nice to have a space that's uniquely you. A good blog is like a scrapbook — have fun, scrutinize less, and enjoy the process. Add a little snippet here, a few blurbs there, perhaps some pressed flowers or a magazine collage. What works is whatever's you.

Sure, there are technical limitations to keep in mind. Namely, keeping track of file sizes and ensuring the media you choose is supported on a wide range of devices. That aside, however, the biggest limitation, the most important and often most challenging bottleneck is just getting started.

Well now, that's old news!

On a flight to visit my cousin on the road to Mycofest © 2026, I decided it would be nice to have a little piece of me on the interwebs that may be shared with those I meet. Nowadays, there are plenty of options for keeping up with the Simpsons. A social media handle, phone number, even good old fashioned e-mail. And for the really determined off-gridders, a signed and stamped letter sent by currior might be the move.

I could to into greater detail about why such options are uninspiring, excessively limited, and burdensome. That, however, is not the point, as ultimately,cto each their preference.

My decision was to spin up a brand new blog using Cloudflare pages. It's free, with certain limitations, save for the $10-20 per year in domain registration fees. And more importantly, I can yap about whatever I want on here!

No weird censorship algorithm discerning whether my use of "finna" or "bet" as conjunctions warrants visibility, no HOA telling my colors don't suit the neighborhood vibe, no goofy AI scan tool being paid by Big Pooper to replicate my swag , though it's only a matter of time, I suspect, before the entire clearnet has been colonized, presuming it hasn't happened already.

Anyway, the idea is, instead of handing out my phone number or social handle, I can tell folks to visit my website if they want to connect more. And hopefully, one day, new friends can show me their websites. How cool! 

Right, right, quickly before I forget, how'd I do it? So, I went to Cloudflare Pages' official website and logged in with a free Cloudflare account. Then I clicked "[create new page]," or something like that, and selected the `Astro` blog template. Astro is particularly cool because it's a minimal typescript blog template not much different from my present (soon to be former) website based on react.js.

After naming the project, I linked the it to my organization's GitHub account for easy access to its source code. Leveraging `git` is not required, though it allows for editing and version control in a way that other methods wouldn't typically go for, and is the de-facto standard in most coding projects. From there, I logged into my GitHub account and started clicking through files in the codebase, editing what seemed like placeholder text to suit my preference, gradually personalizing the site as I go.

I've used plenty of blog site development pathways from WordPress to raw HTML, and haven't much of a preference. These days, I'm caring less for fancy graphic design and more for original self expression, so whatever lets you rock, do that. Astro (short for astronaut) maintains the blog posts as static `.md` markdown files. That means text with simple and powerful styling and formatting options. It's become my ideal way of keeping notes and the method I'd used for previous blogs. For me, using a template like Astro let me launch the site in seconds rather than hours while still allowing the immense flexibility of writing the code from scratch. 

In the past, I have gotten bogged down while attempting to cram the entire site's vision into a slim window of execution. This lead to my disinterest in continuing to work on the project, as it was perpetually in states of incompleteness. Well, I've decided to start anew, taking all that I've learned while integrating myself into a new era. With that, I've written my very first blog post and am so happy to write many more.

Jah
